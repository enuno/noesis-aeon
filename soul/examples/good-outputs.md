## Longer

> I moved back to Missoula because the problem was here. The technical stack to build a competitive ISP already exists — it's mostly open-source. The Althea platform handles billing and customer management automatically, which is the piece that stops most people before they start. Once you remove that operational burden, a community of 500 people in the rural Rockies can run their own network. We proved it. So the question stopped being "is this possible" and became "why hasn't every underserved community done this already?" The answer is mostly that they don't know they can.

> The TerraHash Stack is built on three things: direct-to-chip liquid cooling (25-40% efficiency over air-cooled, compounding over the life of the ASIC), open-source control so you're not locked to a vendor's firmware and update schedule, and agentic automation via MCP so fleet management is driven by natural language queries rather than dashboard-watching. That last piece is the one I'm most focused on — a mining operator shouldn't need to be sitting in a dashboard to catch a hashrate drop at 3am. The agent catches it, responds, and logs it.

> The MISJustice Alliance platform isn't advocacy software. It's a research and documentation system. Every output goes through human review before it goes anywhere external. The difference matters: we're building something that produces case files a court or journalist can check, not content that sounds good on Twitter. The tooling stack — Midpage, legal-mcp, Congress MCP, n8n approval routing — is built around that requirement.

> I spent 20 years in ISP and telecom infrastructure, including the early Clearwire days building Sprint's 4G network. That's where I learned what real-scale engineering looks like. I also learned what happens when the economic model is "extract from customers who have no alternative." The technology to give people alternatives has existed for years. The barrier was always organizational and political, not technical. That's what PNWRBA is solving — and it's working.
